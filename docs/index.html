The Pi Quest

Eight badges. One number.

Every lab on this page can earn you a badge. Progress stays in your browser and nowhere else. Collect all eight to finish the quest.

Labs

Play with the number itself

Every simulation runs live in your browser against a million verified digits. No servers, no libraries, no tracking.

Digit Trainer memory

Type the decimals of π from memory, one digit at a time. Three misses ends the run. 10 digits earns a badge, 25 makes you a memory athlete in training.

Digit Search discovery

Your birthday, your lucky number, any string of digits: if it is in the first million decimals, this finds where it first appears.

Monte Carlo simulation

Throw random darts at a square with a circle inside. The fraction that lands inside the circle estimates π. Watch chance converge on a constant.

Buffon's Needle simulation

Drop needles on a lined floor, as Georges-Louis Leclerc proposed in 1777. The share that crosses a line hides π inside it. The oldest random experiment there is.

Digit Walk art

Each digit 0 to 9 becomes a direction of travel. Following the decimals of π step by step draws a path no one designed. Randomness you can look at.

Digit Spiral art

Thousands of digits wound into a spiral, each digit colored by its value. Structure would show up as bands or streaks. None appear.

Pi Melody sound

The decimals mapped onto a pentatonic scale and played in sequence. Since π never repeats, neither does the tune. Press play and listen to a number.

Digit Census statistics

How often does each digit 0 to 9 actually appear? If π is normal, each should approach ten percent. Compare the first thousand decimals against the first million.

    The state of the art

    Where the limits stood in mid 2026. Sources and the full record table live in the atlas.

    314 trillion
    digits computed by StorageReview and Micron in November 2025, on one server running y-cruncher for 110 days
    300 trillion
    digits verified by Guinness World Records for Kioxia and Linus Media Group in May 2025
    70,000
    decimals recited from memory by Rajveer Meena in 2015, blindfolded, over nearly ten hours
    62 digits
    are all NASA ever needs: 15 decimals steer interplanetary spacecraft, 37 would size the visible universe to within one hydrogen atom
    1706
    the year William Jones first wrote the symbol π for this number, later fixed forever by Euler
    Unproven
    whether every digit sequence appears in π. Normality is believed by everyone and proved by no one

    Formula wall

    Six ways to write the same number

    Each of these is exact. Each was a landmark. The atlas walks through where every one of them comes from.

    π4 =1− 13 + 15 − 17 +⋯

    Madhava, Gregory, Leibniz. The most famous series and the slowest: 300 terms buy you two digits.

    π2 = 21 · 23 · 43 · 45 · 65 · 67 ⋯

    Wallis, 1655. An infinite product from even and odd numbers alone, found before calculus existed.

    π²6 =1+ 12² + 13² + 14² +⋯

    Euler, 1734. The Basel problem: the squares of the whole numbers conspire to produce π.

    eiπ + 1 = 0

    Euler's identity. Five fundamental constants in one line, regularly voted the most beautiful equation.

    π= ∞Σk=0 116k ( 48k+1 − 28k+4 − 18k+5 − 18k+6 )

    Bailey, Borwein, Plouffe, 1995. Extracts any hexadecimal digit of π without computing the ones before it.

    1π =12 ∞Σk=0 (−1)k (6k)! (13591409 + 545140134k) (3k)! (k!)³ 6403203k+3/2

    Chudnovsky brothers, 1988. Fourteen digits per term. Every world record since 1989, and this site's own digits, come from this series.
