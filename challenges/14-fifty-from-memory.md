# Challenge 14: Fifty From Memory

Tier: Apprentice · Time: a week of ten-minute sessions

Ten digits was a party trick. Fifty is a discipline. Nobody holds fifty
decimals by brute repetition; everyone who does it, from hobbyists to the
record holders in [Records](../atlas/06-records.md), uses a system that turns
digits into things a brain actually likes: sounds, images, places, stories.
This challenge is about building yours.

## The task

Memorize the first fifty decimals of π using a real system: the major system
(digits become consonant sounds, sounds become words), a digit-pair image
alphabet, or a story chained across five ten-digit landmarks. Practice in
short sessions across several days, then write all fifty cold.

The fifty, exactly as in [data/pi-1000.txt](../data/pi-1000.txt):

```
3.14159 26535 89793 23846 26433 83279 50288 41971 69399 37510
```

## You have solved it when

- [ ] You can write all fifty decimals cold, no hesitation longer than a breath.
- [ ] You can still do it two days after your last practice.
- [ ] You can name your system and encode a new number with it in under a minute.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Do not memorize fifty digits. Memorize ten chunks of five, and give each
chunk a home. The Digit Trainer's Study view on
[the site](https://amey-thakur.github.io/PI/#labs) already breaks the digits
into these bites with their positions. Anchor chunk one to your front door,
chunk two to your hallway, and walk the route as you recite: the method of
loci, unchanged since ancient Greece because it works.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

There is no answer key to check beyond the digits themselves, so the solution
is the method, worked once. Take the major system, where each digit is a
consonant sound (1 = t/d, 4 = r, 5 = l, 9 = p/b, and so on). The opening
14159 becomes t-r-t-l-p, which you might word as "turtle pib" or split as
"tire" (14) "tail" (15) "pie" (9...). The words are yours; what matters is
that decoding them back to digits is mechanical.

Chunk the fifty as the task shows, five digits to a bite, one image per bite,
ten images on a route you can walk with your eyes closed. Recital then is not
recall of digits at all: it is a stroll past ten pictures, reading each one
back into numbers.

Two honest warnings from everyone who has done this: review beats intensity
(three minutes daily outlives an hour once), and the digits you will scramble
are the ones that look like each other, so give 26433 and 26535 aggressively
different images.

When fifty feels solid, the ladder continues: the trainer on the site scores
runs of any length, the data files go to a million, and
[Records](../atlas/06-records.md) tells you how far this road has been
walked: seventy thousand.

</details>
